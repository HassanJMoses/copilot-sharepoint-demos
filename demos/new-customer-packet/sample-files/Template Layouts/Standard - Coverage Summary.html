What each coverage does, and an example

Business liability covers claims against {{business_name}} for injury or property damage to someone else that you're found responsible for. For example, if a customer slips and falls at your business and is injured, this coverage would pay their medical costs and any judgment against you, up to {{liability_limit}}.

Commercial vehicles covers the vehicles on your schedule while used for business purposes, including liability for injury or damage you cause to others, and physical damage to your own vehicle if you selected that coverage. For example, if one of your vehicles is involved in an accident while on a job, this coverage would respond for the resulting damage and injury claims.

Workers' compensation covers medical costs and a portion of lost wages for an employee injured on the job, at the benefit level required in your state, regardless of who was at fault. For example, if an employee is injured lifting equipment, this coverage pays their medical treatment and a portion of their lost wages while they recover.

Property covers damage to your business property at the address on file from a covered cause of loss, such as fire, windstorm, or theft. For example, if a storm damages your roof, this coverage would pay to repair it, subject to your deductible and the terms of the Property Coverage Form.

What's generally not covered, at a glance

Each coverage form lists its exclusions in full. At a high level: intentional acts, damage to property you own or control, flood and earth movement under the property coverage, and injuries to your own employees under liability coverage (addressed instead by workers' compensation) are generally excluded. Review the applicable coverage form for the complete list.

Glossary

Limit of insurance: the most we will pay under a coverage. Deductible: the amount you pay before coverage responds. Occurrence: an accident, including repeated exposure to similar harmful conditions. Named insured: the business or person shown on your Declarations as the policyholder.

How your premium is determined

Your annual premium of {{annual_premium}} reflects several factors: the coverages and limits you selected, your business type and years in operation, your location, the number of employees and vehicles covered, and your claims history. Adding coverage, a vehicle, or a location during the Policy Period will change your premium; removing one may generate a partial refund.

If you need to make a change

Changes to your coverage, address, vehicles, or contact information should be requested in writing or through your Contoso representative. Most changes take effect on the date we confirm them in writing, not the date you request them, so please allow time before a change is needed.
