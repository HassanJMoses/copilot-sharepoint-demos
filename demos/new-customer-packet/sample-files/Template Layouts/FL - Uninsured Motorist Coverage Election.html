Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage pays for bodily injury to you or your drivers caused by a driver who carries no insurance, or not enough insurance, to cover the damage. Florida law requires that this coverage be offered on every commercial auto policy, at a limit equal to your liability coverage, unless you reject it in writing.

How this coverage works

If one of your covered drivers is injured by an at-fault driver who has no liability insurance, this coverage steps in to pay for their injuries, up to the limit you elect. If the at-fault driver has some insurance but not enough to cover the damages, underinsured motorist coverage pays the difference, up to your elected limit.

This coverage also applies to a hit-and-run accident where the at-fault driver cannot be identified, provided the accident is reported to police within a reasonable time and documented as required by your policy.

Stacking

Unless you elect otherwise, the limits of this coverage do not stack across multiple vehicles on this policy. A single occurrence is subject to a single uninsured/underinsured motorist limit, regardless of how many of your vehicles are involved.

Consequences of rejecting this coverage

If you reject uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage, you and your drivers will have no recourse under this policy for injuries caused by an uninsured or underinsured driver, beyond what your own liability or medical payments coverage may separately provide. Your rejection remains in effect for subsequent renewals of this policy unless you later request the coverage in writing.
